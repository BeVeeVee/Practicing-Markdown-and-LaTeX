Preamble

An's Main Points

In An's recent announcement, "Keeping SpaceHey cozy", the news came to us about the decision to make SpaceHey invite-only for now—I'd like to emphasize again the "for now" portion—but let's gather some points that An addressed in this announcement to clear up the confusion:

Point 1: Spam Accounts

Quoting An in paragraph 3 of the announcement
And while this growth was primarily amazing, there are also downsides to scaling the community to over 2 million people, so fast - especially in regards to spam.

This is true, we've all seen it, we've all experienced it. There has been a large influx of spam accounts, and it's been a problem for a while now. I'm sure that most users have gotten something along the lines of "Hey! I'm open for commissions! Check out my site for commissions!". I've also seen it, the PSA I made about company accounts addresses this PSA BOTTED COMPANY ACCOUNTS. The "2 million people" figure is indeed an exaggeration, as I'm sure will later be mentioned in the comments, the amount of actual active users would be much much lower, but the point still stands that there are a lot of spam accounts, and it's been a problem for a while now.

Point 2: The Decision to Make SpaceHey Invite-Only

Quoting An in paragraph 4 and 5 of the announcement
That's why, after talking to many of you over the past months, I made the difficult decision to make SpaceHey invite-only for now. So starting today, if you're new to SpaceHey and want to join, you will need an invite from someone already on SpaceHey to sign up.

This is one of the main points I see others have a problem with, and I do understand that. But I'd like to point out that An did say "for now". I'm interpreting that this is likely a temporary solution. I'm not sure how long it'll last, or even if it actually is going to be temporary. So from now on, if a new user wants to join, they'll need an invite from someone already on SpaceHey. Remember this: they need an invite from someone already on SpaceHey, it'll come up again later.

Point 3: The Future of SpaceHey

Quoting An in paragraph 6 of the announcement
This will allow us to grow the platform more sustainably, by focusing on quality over quantity, putting safety and personal connections over rapid growth, staying true to our tagline: SpaceHey - a space for friends.

It's somewhat true that this is a way to grow the platform in a more controlled manner. There have been multiple times when the site has been down for one reason or another, even multiple times in a day. When a platform grows rapidly, so should the server architecture. Slowing down SpaceHey's growth in this manner could fix the bottlenecks and allow for a more sustainable user experience. And to add, unchecked growth turns any social media platform into a cesspool of spam, bots, and worse corporate, ad-infested platforms. I don't want SpaceHey to turn into that, and I'm sure most of you don't either.

Community Concerns and My Counters

Now, let's address concerns that most of the community have brought up, both in the comments of An's announcements, and in public blog posts. I've aggregated some of the common ones:

Concern 1: Bots inviting other bots

While this is a valid concern, I feel like this has no legs to stand on. I'd like to point out the obvious hole in this argument:

The Obvious Hole
SpaceHey was already open-registration, meaning that anyone, AND I MEAN ANYONE, could sign up.

Before this update, anyone could join in, me, you, my little brother, my little sister, the printer in the office, a mega corporation, ANYONE. So the argument that bots could just invite other bots is moot. Creating an invite-only system solves this and many other points, such as:

Paper Trail
Stated on the SpaceHey Invite Page is the following:
Here you can create Invite Codes for your Friends to join SpaceHey.
You currently have (this many) invite codes and may get more from time to time.
Important: People using your invites will be able to see who invited them.
Created invite links expire after 3 weeks when not used.

I'd like to point out: "Important: People using your invites will be able to see who invited them." Someone like An wouldn't implement such a system without being able to track back who invited who. If a spammer/scammer/bot invites another spammer/scammer/bot, there is such a way to trail back who it was. And to add to this point, another platform, who many might know as Discord, also has such a system in place. Moderators on servers can see who created an invite link and who joined using that invite link, and can even disable that invite link if they wanted to. This would be a way to hold the inviter accountable for the invitee.

Concern 2: Gatekeeping

I've read many replies that the invite-only system locks out introverts or genuine people who just don't have friends here yet. I understand this, I empathize, being a shut-in myself. But then there's another platform I'd like to point out for this point that also has a thriving community AND an invite system: Archive of Our Own (AO3)

AO3's Invite System
AO3's invite policy is actually stricter than SpaceHey's, requiring you to email them, where you'll then be added into an automated queue, and wait for weeks—even months—to even allow the system to register you. And yet, AO3 is still a thriving community, with millions of users and works. The invite-only system is not a barrier to entry for genuine people who want to join the community, it's a barrier to entry for bots and spammers.

The fact that we even get a regenerating pool of invite codes seems way more generous by comparison. Following this, the invite page also states that you can get more invite codes from time to time, which seems to be a way to reward active users, and also a way to ensure that there are always invite codes available for genuine people who want to join the community.

Concern 3: The Moderation Issue

Lastly, a huge chunk of the community is concerned about the moderation. And while I haven't experienced any problems myself, I can infer from context that there is a genuine issue with inappropriate content slipping through.

However, this problem has nothing to do with the invite system. The point of the update was to slow down and make sure that the platform grown in a more manageable way, and to curb the influx of spam accounts. The moderation issue is a separate issue that needs to be addressed separately.

My Opinion

This is not the "final nail in the coffin" for SpaceHey, and I feel like it might even be the very thing that could keep the platform alive without it turning into a spammy, bot-infested, corporate ad platform. I understand the concerns, I understand the frustrations, but I also understand the reasoning behind the decision. I'm willing to give it a chance, and see how it goes. I'm sure there will be some bumps along the way, but I'm hopeful that this will lead to a better experience for everyone in the long run. If you'd like to talk about anything, or have any points to add, please feel free to comment them for an open, respectful, honest discussion.
